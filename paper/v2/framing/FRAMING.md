## 2. Abstract (188 words)

Under a fixed wall clock an agent's score saturates in its token budget, so a change either adds tokens or changes the curve, and noise hides which. Our offline, one-GPU ARC-AGI-3 entry (public notebook plus 19 changes) scored 40.09 on the public leaderboard (rank 9 of 4,253 on 2026-10-13; one draw, best of nine). Byte-identical runs on our 25-game development replica differ by up to 22 levels (SD 8.25, CI 5.3 to 18.2); two runs per arm resolve 23.1 levels, and a one-run rule promotes a null change 17 to 20% of the time. The replica sits at 95% of its fitted ceiling, so +11% serving tokens are worth about 4 of 124 levels, below detection. Yielding after 8,192 generated tokens, not 2,048, coincides with 124.0 levels over nine runs against 111.5 over 21 (+12.5, CI +9.2 to +15.8; +12.3 without one unadjudicated arm), which the model reads as moving the curve. Extra tokens could explain about 5 of those levels and the measured mechanism frees 2.3% of GPU time: the cause is open. A fourth draft step loses (acceptance 0.275, break-even 0.379). Replica numbers are development results.

## 3. Contributions (four; each refutable; each with its section)

1. **Instrument and gate (Sec. 3).** Six byte-identical pairs of our 25-game replica put the run-to-run SD at 8.25 levels (6 df, CI 5.3 to 18.2; 7.82 on 13 df when pooled with an older stack). Two runs per arm resolve 23.1 levels, and a one-run rule promotes a null change 17 to 20% of the time. We gate on predeclared mechanism counters and use levels only as a veto. Refuted by further identical pairs whose pooled SD falls outside about 5 to 18.
2. **A budget curve that classes levers (Sec. 4).** A saturating score-versus-token curve, built from recorded level attempts, puts the replica at 95% of its stuck-mass ceiling with elasticity 0.22 on score and 0.31 on levels (0.45 to 0.60 in two constructed harder worlds). It prices our +11% serving tokens at about 4 of 124 levels, which takes 12 to 48 runs per arm to see. Refuted by a token-only change of +26% (predicted about +10 levels; 2 to 9 runs per arm resolve it) that lands far from that.
3. **A lever with an open cause (Sec. 6).** Yielding after 8,192 generated tokens instead of 2,048 coincides with +12.5 levels (nine runs against 21; +12.3 and +13.5 after dropping one unadjudicated arm and two unshipped arms). About 70% fewer resume openers (2.3% of GPU time) and extra tokens in seven of the nine runs (about 5 levels on the curve) leave the cause open. An interleaved same-VM test at 8 runs per arm, not yet run, that shows a gap under +4 levels would count against the effect size (probability 0.02 if the effect is +12.5, SD 8.25 taken as known).
4. **Speculative decoding under sampling (Sec. 5).** At temperature 0.7 a fourth MTP draft step raises accept length 9.8% but lowers decode throughput 4.2%: its measured acceptance, 0.275, is below the 0.379 break-even and below the 0.350 a geometric extrapolation gives, and the step pays only below about 8 running requests (extrapolated below 11; we serve 14). A refit drafter adds 4.8% accept length and 5.5% throughput. The sampling ceiling is Bebop's; ours is the measured gap. Refuted by per-position K=4 acceptance above 0.379 on this stack (not yet measured).

What we report and do not claim, placed directly under the contributions: the leaderboard result is the best of nine live draws and one draw of its configuration; the two draws after the yield change beat all seven before it (exact p = 1/36) but each carries other changes, so we attribute neither draw, nor the 5.75-point step between them, to any lever. We do not claim that the yield caused the whole gap, that serving has no effect, that replica numbers are held out, or anything about the effect of the QSA ring fix.

## 6. Writing rules for section writers

Voice and tense
- "We", active voice, short sentences. Past tense for what we ran and measured; present tense for definitions and for what a figure shows. First sentence of each section is its answer.
- Say "levels" or "score", never "performance". Quote levels (0.31) and score (0.22) elasticities separately; never convert between them.
- Plain nouns, five defined terms only: replica, level, draw, yield, ceiling. No arm codes in the main text (`l1a1y8sv2r3p`, `p448`, `hm32` appear only in Appendix A and the claims map). Say "the shipped configuration".
- Every result sits next to n, an interval or SD, and which test (one- or two-sided, exact or bootstrap). Every MDE prints the SD behind it.
- "Significant" only with a test. "Novel" never, except for a measurement we can show is first (and then say "we found no earlier measurement").

Hedging dictionary
- Yield: "coincides with", "is associated with"; never "caused", "improves", "raised the plateau" in the abstract or Sec. 1. The curve's classification may be stated in Sec. 4 and 6 as "the model classes it as a ceiling shift (+5.2 points, CI +3.2 to +11.2, three runs per pool)". Safe headline: "changed the curve, not the position on it".
- Serving: "unresolved, not zero"; "worth about 4 of 124 levels on the curve"; never "no effect", "did not help", "no gain" as a finding.
- Strong claims use the indicative; moderate claims name their limit in the same sentence; weak claims use "is consistent with".
- Inferences are written as "we estimate" or "at the curve's own elasticity", never as measurements.

Required labels (every occurrence class)

## 8. Pre-emptive replies (the sentence the paper gives)

1. **The cause of the yield gain is open.** "We report +12.5 levels as a difference between run groups that were not interleaved. The nine runs also generate 15% more tokens (17% for the seven with serving riders), which the curve prices at about 5 of the 12.5 levels. The two rider-free runs generated the same tokens as their control (-0.2%) and were ahead from 100K tokens per game, but they are a selected pair. The measured mechanism, about 70% fewer resume openers, frees 2.3% of GPU time. An interleaved same-VM test at 8 runs per arm resolves 11.6 levels at SD 8.25 and would settle it; it has not been run."
2. **A predicted null cannot lose, and "serving buys little" is absence of evidence.** "We never write that serving has no effect. At elasticity 0.31 on levels, +11% tokens is worth about 4 of 124 levels and +26% about 10; seeing +11% takes 12 to 48 runs per arm against 2 in our gates, so the model can lose and the gates could not have seen it. Counters (tokens per second CV 1.29% to 3.23% against 7.31% for levels) verify that a lever did what it claims, not what it earned."
3. **Six pairs, a heavy tail, an order effect.** "The SD rests on six pairs (6 df, CI 5.3 to 18.2) and two pairs hold 91% of the sum of squares; the robust SD is 5.2, the SD about the mean gap (9.2) is 5.6, and the two eras pooled give 7.82 on 13 df (CI 5.67 to 12.59). At 2 vs 2 the MDE is 14.7, 23.1 and 50.9 at SD 5.2, 8.25 and 18.2; a one-run rule still promotes a null change 8.9% of the time at the robust SD. The yield tests are permutation tests that use no SD."
4. **These are development-set numbers.** "Every replica number comes from the 25 public games we developed on and is labelled so; the simulator check is leave-one-run-out, not held out. The only held-out number is the leaderboard score, which covers half of the 110 hidden games; the private half is unknown."
5. **40.09 is a maximum, and one draw.** "It is the best of nine live draws and one draw of its configuration. We list all eleven completed draws and explain both zeros (field-wide Kaggle events: 63 of 64 and 48 of 61 runs in the 00:00 to 00:20Z wave ended early). The seven earlier draws average 29.25 (SD 1.58); the expected maximum of nine draws is 1.48 SD above the mean (+2.3 at SD 1.58, +5.8 at the field SD 3.93). The 1/36 comparison is a rank test across different, time-ordered configurations, so it ranks the stack and attributes nothing; the 5.75-point step is inside draw noise (p 0.010, 0.24, 0.30 at SD 1.58, 3.47, 3.93)."
6. **The curve is a simulator and "ceiling shift" restates "more levels".** "The curve resamples recorded attempts. Leave-one-run-out errors at matched tokens are -1.7 to +1.8 levels, but end to end it is 4.9 levels low (se 1.5), as large as the 4-level effect it predicts; the ceiling rests on a censoring rule (61.0 against 55.8 across pools, ratio 0.95 in both); the harder-world elasticities (0.45 to 0.60) are two constructions. The +5.2 ceiling gap (CI +3.2 to +11.2) comes from three runs per pool and classifies the effect; the evidence for the effect is the 9 against 21 comparison."

Our contributions are the measured gaps on this stack and the protocol, not the ideas."

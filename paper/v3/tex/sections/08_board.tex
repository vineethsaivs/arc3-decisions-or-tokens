\paragraph{One agent's draws spread widely.}
The same agent in two serving variants, one with a draft vocabulary that should leave the output distribution unchanged, averaged 38.43 over three draws and 31.15 over four (Appendix~\ref{app:draws}). % V3-B4
Five later draws with a keepalive that acts only while the server boots averaged 35.04, between the two, and three draws of a build with one more game in flight averaged 34.35. % V3-W16 V3-W24
The variants were drawn in separate stretches of days, so a calendar or Kaggle-node effect plus chance is the economical reading.
The same calendar term applies to every time-ordered comparison here, including the board step after the yield change and the development-run comparison of Sec.~\ref{sec:yield}.

\paragraph{The board ranks the stack and attributes nothing.}
The seven live draws before the yield change average 29.25 and the 21 after it 34.96, three of them an adapter's (Sec.~\ref{sec:finetune}). % V3-B5 G2 V3-W30
Those are different, time-ordered configurations, each carrying other changes, so the step is a trend and not a lever's effect.

\paragraph{How much of the best draw is selection.}
If the first eight live draws after the yield change shared one mean, the best of eight would sit about 1.4 SD above it, near 40.3 (Appendix~\ref{app:draws}). % V3-B6
Resampling the twelve valid draws of the agent's family, whose record closed on 2026-10-28, gives an expected best of twelve of 40.33 (Figure~\ref{fig:selection}). % V3-W16
So 40.98 is what a maximum of this family looks like, not evidence of a higher mean; with the seventeen valid draws the expected best is 39.6 to 40.7 across three models (up to 41.1 for 21 draws), and the chance that the best of 17 reaches 40.98 is 0.17 to 0.64 (Appendix~\ref{app:noisegates}). % V3-W52
This is the optimizer's curse, and shrinking toward the family mean is its standard correction \citep{smith2006optimizer}; the expected maximum differs from the best member's mean \citep{mollersen2023multiplicity}. A harness on ARC-AGI-3 picked after the fact shows it: its first score sat 5 points above its ten-run mean \citep{lehmann2026harness}.
Picking two Final Submissions by public score is selection among noisy draws of one build, not the adaptive holdout reuse studied by \citet{blum2015ladder}; \citet{roelofs2019meta} found little of that across more than 100 Kaggle competitions, so the bias here is the maximum of noisy draws, not overfitting. % G8

\paragraph{What to expect on the private half.}
Within a configuration the public and private halves of a draw correlate 0.00 to 0.53 on our model, so a draw picked by its public score should be expected to score near the family mean on the private half, not near its public score. % G8
The agent's seventeen valid draws and its zero of unknown cause average 32.67 together. Our expectation for the private score of the two marked draws, both of one build, is the family mean 34.6 to that build's shrunk mean 37.0, about 35 to 37, with a per-draw SD of about 3.4; the build effect itself is not established (ANOVA $p$ 0.11). A coding-agent study sees the same shrinkage: the gain kept about a third on later data \citep{arino2026identical}. Two other entrants report their own repeated board draws at SD 3.2 (13 draws over five variants) and about 3.8, next to our 3.4 \citep{rodrigues2026arc3,vilot8882026arc3}. % V3-W30 V3-W52

\subsection{First-run comparisons replayed}\label{app:gates}

Table~\ref{tab:gatehist} replays our first-run comparisons under one rule: beating the control run by 10 levels or more is a win, trailing by 10 or more a loss, anything else a tie. % B7
The replay is post hoc. At the time each comparison used the control then at hand, predeclared counters and often more than one run, so the table shows what a one-run rule would have called. Our decisions used more evidence. % B7

The rule calls 2 wins, 6 losses and 9 ties. % B7
Both wins shipped on a verified mechanism and held up on replication (pairs 122 and 106, 128 and 124). % B7
Every control in a loss call was the higher run of its byte-identical pair: 122 of 122 and 106, 123 of 123 and 101, 128 of 128 and 124. The six loss calls rest on three control runs (122 three times, 123 twice, 128 once), so this is three facts. % B7
Against the pooled control five of the six losses shrink to 8 levels or fewer. Row 16 stays at $-11$, but that run failed its own mechanism check and lost its server to an out-of-memory error in the last minutes of the window. % B7 B12
Row 16 is also the one run that differs from a treated run in the yield line alone, with all other changes in place: 115 levels against 119 for row 17. % D2
Row 12 was a loss call ($-10$) that we shipped on its mechanism, and a tie against the pooled control. % B7

On the older stack five arms were promoted on a first or lucky run. Their advantage over the control family was $+7.0$, $+9.0$, $+11.0$, $+4.0$ and $+9.0$ levels on that run and $+2.5$, $+1.0$, $+0.7$, $-0.5$ and $+7.0$ after the byte-identical replicate: means $+8.0$ and $+2.1$ (73\% shrinkage). % B7
Two of the five were withdrawn the same or the next day, and one survived its replicate. % B7
